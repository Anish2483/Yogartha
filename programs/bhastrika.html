What is Bhastrika?

The Breath of Fire

Bhastrika, meaning “bellows breath,” is a classical yogic pranayama (breathing practice) in which the breath is forcefully and rhythmically pumped in and out, like the bellows of a blacksmith’s forge. It is one of the most powerful breathing practices in the Hatha Yoga tradition.

This vigorous practice rapidly fills the blood with oxygen while expelling carbon dioxide, generating significant inner heat (tapas) and powerfully activating the energy system. Bhastrika clears the respiratory tract, stimulates the digestive fire, and awakens a vibrant, alive quality in the entire system.

When practiced with proper guidance and correct technique, Bhastrika is a transformative tool for physical vitality, mental clarity, and the awakening of deeper dimensions of inner energy. It must always be learned from a qualified teacher and practiced with awareness.

Benefits

Why Practice Bhastrika

Energizes the System

Rapidly floods the body with oxygen and activates the energy system, creating a surge of vitality and aliveness that is immediately felt.

Clears the Respiratory Tract

The forceful breathing clears the nasal passages, bronchial tubes, and lungs of accumulated congestion and stagnation, improving respiratory function.

Improves Lung Capacity

Regular practice significantly increases lung capacity and respiratory efficiency — beneficial for general health, stamina, and athletic performance.

Ignites Inner Fire

Generates powerful inner heat (agni) that stimulates digestion, burns toxins and impurities, and awakens the body’s metabolic intelligence.

Sharpens the Mind

The rapid oxygenation of the brain produces a state of heightened mental alertness, clarity, and sharpness that persists well after the practice.

Awakens Energy

Acts as a powerful awakening of dormant energy in the system — preparing the practitioner for deeper pranayama and meditative states.

The Practice

What Bhastrika Involves

01
Forceful Rhythmic Breathing

A specific technique of forceful inhalation and exhalation, performed at a controlled pace, building progressively in speed and intensity.

02
Retention (Kumbhaka)

After a round of Bhastrika, the breath is retained for a specific period — a critical component that amplifies the practice’s benefits.

03
Bandhas (Energy Locks)

Specific energy locks are applied during retention to contain and direct the tremendous energy generated during the practice.

04
Preparation & Warm-Up

Proper preparatory practices and warm-up of the respiratory system are essential before Bhastrika to ensure safety and maximum benefit.

05
Must Be Learned from a Teacher

Due to its intensity, Bhastrika must be learned directly from a qualified teacher. Self-practice without guidance can lead to adverse effects.

06
Contraindications & Safety

Not suitable during pregnancy or for those with high blood pressure, heart conditions, or certain other medical conditions. Reeta will assess individually.
